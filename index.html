From slides to Canvas: your presentation video in three steps

You already made the slides. This guide walks you through recording yourself presenting with your face on screen, uploading the video to YouTube, and posting the link in Canvas so your instructor and classmates can watch it.

1Record your presentation with your face visible

Pick the tool you made your slides in. Each one can show your webcam in a small window while you talk through your slides. The goal is one video file (MP4) or a recording you can download.

Pick your tool, then press record!

PowerPoint (desktop app)

  1. Open your slides and go to the Record tab, then choose From Beginning.
  2. In the recording window, make sure the camera and microphone are turned on (check the icons at the bottom right). Pick the right camera and mic in the settings if needed.
  3. Press the red record button, wait for the 3 second countdown, and present. Use the arrows to move between slides.
  4. Press stop when you finish, then close the recording window.
  5. Go to File, Export, Create a Video. Choose Full HD (1080p), keep "Use Recorded Timings and Narrations," and save as MP4.
Exporting can take several minutes. Keep PowerPoint open until the progress bar at the bottom of the window finishes.

Canva

  1. Open your presentation and click Share, then look for Present and record (you may need to click See all).
  2. Click Go to recording studio and allow Canva to use your camera and microphone in the browser.
  3. Pick your camera and mic, then click Start recording. Your face appears in a circle on the slide.
  4. Move through the slides with the arrows. Click End recording when you finish.
  5. Click Download to save the MP4 to your computer. You will upload this file to YouTube.
You can drag your face bubble so it does not cover text on a slide.

Google Slides

  1. Open your slides. If you see a Rec button at the top right, click it, then choose Record new video.
  2. Allow camera and microphone access. Your camera view appears next to the slide.
  3. Click the red button to start, present your slides, then click Stop.
  4. Click Save to Drive. Open the video in Google Drive and download it as a file.
The Rec button is not available on every Google account. If you do not see it, use the Zoom tab instead, or download your slides as a PowerPoint file (File, Download, Microsoft PowerPoint) and follow the PowerPoint steps.

Zoom (by yourself, no one else in the meeting)

  1. Open your slides on your computer first. Then open the Zoom desktop app and click New Meeting with your video on.
  2. Click Share Screen, choose the window with your slides, and click Share. Your camera stays in a small floating window.
  3. Start your slideshow, then click More (or Record) and choose Record on this Computer.
  4. Present. When you finish, stop recording and click End, End Meeting for All.
  5. Zoom converts the recording and opens a folder (usually Documents/Zoom). The MP4 file inside is your video.
Choose "Record on this Computer," not cloud recording, so you get an MP4 file you can upload to YouTube.

Before you hit record

Quick tips first!

Light in front of youFace a window or lamp. Light behind you turns your face into a shadow.
Camera at eye levelStack books under your laptop and look at the camera, not the screen, when you talk.
Do a 10 second testRecord a short clip, play it back, and check your voice volume and framing.
Quiet and clean screenSilence your phone and close chats, email, and notifications.
Check the time limitPractice once with a timer so your video fits the assignment length.
Mistakes are finePause, take a breath, and keep going. You do not need a perfect take.

2Upload the video to YouTube as Unlisted

Unlisted means anyone with the link can watch, but the video does not show up in search or on your channel. That is exactly what you want for class.

Your link is the key to your video.

  1. Go to studio.youtube.com and sign in with a Google account. If YouTube asks you to create a channel the first time, follow the prompts.
  2. Click Create (top right), then Upload videos, and choose your MP4 file.
  3. Details: give it a clear title, for example "Your Name, Course Number, Presentation Title." A description is optional.
  4. Audience: choose No, it's not made for kids. Click Next.
  5. Click Next on Video elements and Checks.
  6. Visibility: choose Unlisted, then click Save.
  7. Copy the video link shown in the window (it looks like https://youtu.be/...). Paste it somewhere safe, such as a note.
Test before you submit: open your link in a private or incognito browser window where you are not signed in. If the video plays there, your instructor can watch it too.

3Post the link in Canvas

Your instructor may ask for the video in an assignment, a discussion, or a page. Pick the way that matches where you are submitting. Embedding shows the player right inside Canvas, which is the friendliest for viewers.

Time to deliver your work!

Option A. Submit as a Website URL (assignments)

  1. Open the assignment and click Start Assignment.
  2. Choose the Website URL tab. If you only see "File Upload" or "Text Entry," use one of the other options.
  3. Paste your YouTube link and click Submit Assignment.
This is the simplest option. Your instructor sees the link and can open it in a new tab.

Option B. Embed the video player (text entry, discussions, pages)

  1. Get the embed code: on your YouTube video, click Share, then Embed, then Copy. Or paste your link into the embed maker below.
  2. In Canvas, open the text box (Text Entry submission, a discussion reply, or a page in edit mode).
  3. Click the Embed icon (it looks like a small cloud). If you do not see it, click the three dots ⋮ at the end of the toolbar to show more icons. You can also use Insert, Embed in the menu bar.
  4. Paste the embed code into the box and click Submit. The video player appears in the editor.
  5. Click Post Reply, Save, or Submit Assignment.
The YouTube app under the plug (Apps) icon searches public YouTube only, so it will not find your Unlisted video. Use the embed code instead.

Option C. Make a clickable text link

  1. In any Canvas text box, type a short label, for example "Watch my presentation video."
  2. Highlight the label, click the Link icon (a chain), and choose External Link.
  3. Paste your YouTube link and click Done.
  4. Post or submit.
Pasting the plain link into a text box also works. A labeled link just looks tidier.

Option D. Paste code in the HTML editor

  1. In the Canvas text box, click the </> icon at the bottom right of the editor to switch to the HTML editor.
  2. Paste the embed code (or the link code) from the maker below where you want the video.
  3. Click </> again to switch back and check that the player shows up, then post or submit.
Use this if the Embed icon is missing in your course, or if you want to add text above and below the video.

Fix a problem

The most common snags, and what to do.

Let's investigate.

My instructor sees "Video unavailable" or "This video is private."

The video is set to Private. In YouTube Studio, open Content, click the visibility next to your video, change it to Unlisted, and save. The link stays the same, so you do not need to resubmit.

The browser cannot find my camera or microphone.

Click the lock or camera icon in the address bar and set Camera and Microphone to Allow, then reload. Close other apps that might be using the camera (Zoom, Teams, FaceTime). On a Mac, also check System Settings, Privacy and Security, Camera and Microphone.

The embedded player does not show up in Canvas.

Make sure you pasted the full embed code that starts with <iframe, not the plain link. If the Embed box does not accept it, use the HTML editor option. You can always fall back to a plain link.

The upload is stuck or very slow.

Stay on the upload page until it reaches 100 percent. Use a stronger Wi-Fi connection if you can. Large files from screen recorders can be big; exporting at 1080p instead of 4K helps.

I made a mistake in the video. Do I have to start over?

Small cuts at the beginning or end can be trimmed in YouTube Studio under Editor, Trim and cut. For bigger changes, record again and upload the new video, then update your Canvas submission with the new link if resubmission is allowed.

The Zoom recording never appeared.

Zoom converts the recording after the meeting ends. Wait for the conversion window to finish. Then look in Documents/Zoom, or open the Zoom app and check Meetings, Recorded.

Still stuck? Write down what you tried and where it stopped, then ask your instructor or a classmate. A screenshot of the screen helps a lot.
